# Internal review, October 2026

**This is not an audit.** It is the project's own adversarial review of the draft as it stood on 7 October 2026, carried out in three rounds before asking anyone outside to look, so that outside reviewers do not spend their time on what the project could have found itself. In the first round four reviewers worked independently, one each on the on-chain program, the cryptographic design, the client and offline tool, and the website and alert service. The second round reviewed the first round's fixes. The third started again with different questions: a line-by-line comparison of specification and program, the ways an honest user loses funds or gets stuck, every published claim against the code, and the cryptography with its numbers recomputed. Each was asked to break it, and to demonstrate what they claimed with a working test. They share an author's blind spots with the code they reviewed. No independent party has checked the findings or the fixes.

Severity is the project's own judgement. "Test" names the regression test that reproduces the original attack and now requires it to fail.

## Program and protocol

| ID | Severity | Finding | Resolution | Test |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| P-1 | Critical | **Spent markers were shared by every vault.** A marker's address came from the root alone, `initialize` accepted any root, and `recover` retires the displaced operational root without a signature under it. Anyone could create a vault holding a victim's public `op_root` or `rec_root`, recover it with their own key, and permanently block the victim's `announce` and `recover`. A day-key thief could block cancel-by-recovery during the waiting period and collect when it ended. | Markers are derived from the vault address and the root. `initialize` takes no marker accounts. | `isolation.rs`: `retiring_a_root_in_one_vault_does_not_retire_it_in_another`, `two_vaults_may_hold_the_same_root`, `a_marker_from_another_vault_is_refused` |
| P-2 | Critical | **A vault's address did not commit to its keys.** The address came from a caller-chosen identifier, and `initialize` was first come, first served. ## Design review

After the three rounds above, three further reviewers were asked about the design rather than the code: one to rule on every open question in PROTOCOL.md, one on the choice of signature scheme, and one on whether the design delivers what the product claims. Their conclusions, and what was done:

| ID | Conclusion | Action |
|---|---|---|
| D-1 | A token withdrawal for a mint the program cannot handle, or with a mistaken idea of the token's decimals, was only discovered at execution, or not at all. | **Changed.** The announcement states the decimals and names the mint; the program checks both before any key is retired. `program.rs`: `a_token_announcement_is_checked_against_its_mint` |
| D-2 | Seven days was too long for a record that cannot execute to hold a vault. | **Changed.** The window is the waiting period again, with a one-day floor. `state.rs` |
| D-3 | The upgrade authority must be decided before an audit is worth paying for; for this project the right answer is an immutable program. | **Decided** and written in DEPLOYMENT.md. Not yet carried out: nothing is deployed. |
| D-4 | The rule that only signed roots are marked (P-11) was examined for a counterexample and none was found. The invariants it rests on: the epoch only increases and a vault is never closed or re-created; an announcement must name the current epoch and be signed under the live root; a root becomes live in an epoch only through that epoch's own authority; a marker exists only for a root that signed. | None needed. The vault must remain unclosable. |
| D-5 | The offline tool should not take the network's identity on trust from a file. | **Partly.** The tool recognises mainnet's genesis hash itself, labels everything else a test network, and refuses mainnet in this release. Pinning a mainnet program id waits for there to be one. |
| D-6 | **Signature scheme.** The current scheme has no known practical weakness, but it is not a standard construction, has no published proof, and its message hash relies on SHA-256 collision resistance in a way that is safe only because of where an unpredictable field happens to sit in the message. The recommendation is to replace it with LM-OTS (RFC 8554, `LMOTS_SHA256_N32_W8`) before the design is frozen: the same shape and nearly the same cost, a randomised message hash, published proofs and third-party test vectors. | **Not done. This is the largest open item.** It replaces the only cryptographic code that runs on-chain and should be agreed with the outside cryptographer before it is written. |
| D-7 | **Product.** With no waiting period (then the default), a Bunker protected against a malicious signature in the wallet and against a leaked seed phrase, and not against malware on the device that holds the day key, a phished day key, or a compromise of the website. The one defence against those is the waiting period. Proposed: withdrawals to addresses fixed when the vault is made are instant, everything else waits. | **Changed, by the owner's decision.** A vault names up to four trusted wallets in its creation data, so they are part of its address; withdrawals to them, or to their associated token accounts, open at once; anything else waits the vault's waiting period. The tool starts on 24 hours and requires a statement of the choice to be accepted. `isolation.rs`: `a_stolen_day_key_can_only_pay_trusted_wallets_without_waiting`; `program.rs`: `a_token_withdrawal_is_instant_only_to_a_trusted_wallets_associated_account`; `custody3.spec.ts`. Not done: holding the day key by passkey by default. |
| D-8 | Other recommendations: events emitted by the program so that others can run watchers; more than one pending withdrawal, since a vault with a long waiting period and several assets takes that period once per asset to empty; a recovery packet issued in advance so that cancelling does not need the recovery kit; an exhaustive bounded model. | **The exhaustive model is done** (`programs/bunker3/tests/exhaustive.rs`): every sequence up to twelve actions deep with an adversarial operational signer, checked to fail on the flawed rule of the second draft. It models one vault; isolation between vaults stays with the VM tests. The other three are open. |

## Review of trusted destinations

The trusted-destination change (D-7) was given to a fresh reviewer to attack before it was merged. No way was found to get an instant withdrawal to an unlisted address, to change the list, or to bypass it in the program; this was run against the real token and associated-token-account programs with a mint the attacker created. Four issues in the clients and documents were found and fixed:

| ID | Severity | Finding | Resolution |
|---|---|---|---|
| T-1 | Medium | The site compared the network's trusted list with the day key's only when a Bunker was opened. A connection that lied afterwards could put its own address among the "trusted" buttons and have the review call it trusted. The program would still make it wait, but the owner would have signed believing otherwise. | Every read of the vault is held to the day key's list and waiting period, on the site and in the command-line client. |
| T-2 | Low | An address controlled by a program could be listed as trusted, and then never paid, because the clients send only to wallets. The list cannot be changed. | The offline tool accepts only ordinary wallet addresses as trusted. |
| T-3 | Low | A stolen day key can now move assets irreversibly to trusted addresses, where before everything could be cancelled. The tool recommended an exchange deposit address, which may not credit an unlisted token or a small amount. | The advice now says a hardware wallet and explains the risk; the threat model states the assumption. |
| T-4 | Low | The specification gave the execution window in terms of the vault's waiting period; the code uses the wait that applied to that withdrawal. | Specification corrected. |

## What was tried and held

Enabling mainnet writes through environment or forged configuration; reaching a non-allowlisted RPC method through the proxy; script injection through `/check?a=`, the not-found page and SVG assets; substituting instructions between simulation and wallet signing; an RPC redirecting a withdrawal's destination; crafted key files and public files; secrets in published files; and, in the program, type confusion between account kinds, account aliasing, cross-role proof substitution, replay, double execution, and redirecting a token transfer.

## What this review does not establish

That the design is sound; that the Winternitz construction is correctly chosen or implemented beyond agreement between two ports; that the fixes introduce no new flaw; that any of the estimates above are right.

The record of this review argues for caution about itself. Two critical flaws survived every earlier test because no test put two vaults in one world. The fix for one high-severity flaw (P-8) was incomplete and was caught only by a later round (P-11). The fix for another (W-6) missed three paths. Each round found something the previous one had not. There is no reason to think the third round found the last of it.
